Week 08 / Lesson 06

Read a vector as a representation

Predict simple similarity scores and reject incompatible embeddings.

An embedding model maps text into a list of numbers. Dimensions are the count of numbers, not a count of words. For a learned model, individual coordinates usually do not have human-readable names. Similar geometry may reflect semantic relationships, but a score does not prove an answer is correct.

A deliberately tiny geometry

For intuition only, draw [1,0] pointing right and [0,1] pointing up. Cosine similarity measures alignment by dividing their dot product by their lengths. A dot product multiplies corresponding coordinates and adds. For [1,0] and [1,1], the numerator is 1 and the lengths are 1 and √2, giving about 0.707. Perpendicular vectors have score 0; identical nonzero direction gives 1 even when length differs.

Read cosine in examples.py. zip pairs coordinates, sum adds products, and math.sqrt gives square root. The dimension check happens before zip, which would otherwise silently stop at the shorter input. A zero vector has zero length and cannot be normalized. These are practical data checks, not interview exercises.

Model compatibility is more than vector length

Two models can emit the same number of values while using different coordinate systems. Comparing their outputs is still invalid for this pipeline. Keep model name, revision, dimension, normalization and chunk-policy version with the collection configuration. Changing the model requires re-embedding stored text and queries together. Do not pad a 384-dimensional vector to fit a different collection.

Guided exercise: predict cosine([2,0],[1,0]) and cosine([1,0],[-1,0]) before evaluating them:

.venv/bin/python -c "from practice.examples import cosine; print(cosine([2,0],[1,0])); print(cosine([1,0],[-1,0]))"
Hint

Scaling length does not change direction; reversing direction does.

Explained answer

The results are 1.0 and −1.0. Neither score is a calibrated probability. A 0.9 nearest neighbour can still be irrelevant when the corpus lacks an answer.

Independent variation: predict the error for one zero vector and for unequal dimensions, then run each in a separate command. Log the failure and explain why rejecting bad input is more useful than assigning it a misleading score.

Choose a model for the task

It is a compact starting point for English fictional policy documents, not proof it fits every language or domain. In decisions.md, compare corpus language, license, size/download, CPU latency and truncation behavior against your task.
