Week 10 / Lesson 06

Survive duplicate and dead jobs

Describe a retry-safe job transition and a controlled replay.

Queues decouple an API's response from slow work. That also separates “accepted” from “finished.” A worker may crash after changing storage but before acknowledging the job. A queue can then deliver the same work again. Reliability must account for duplicates rather than assuming exactly one execution.

A concrete failure timeline

job upload-7/v2: queued → running
worker upserts chunk upload-7/v2/0
worker crashes before acknowledgement
queue delivers upload-7/v2 again
replacement worker upserts the same stable chunk ID
replacement marks job succeeded and acknowledges

Idempotent means repeated application has the same intended effect. Stable IDs plus an upsert can avoid duplicate chunks. They do not automatically prevent double billing or duplicate emails. A deduplication key should include tenant, document version and operation; reusing an old key for changed content is a conflict. Store the key and result durably and atomically with the protected side effect where possible. An in-memory set disappears on restart and cannot coordinate multiple workers.

A state transition is a change with preconditions: queued→running claims work; running→succeeded records completion; temporary failure→retry-wait schedules another attempt; permanent failure or exhausted attempts→dead. A dead job is held for inspection, not silently discarded. Preserve safe error type, attempt count and operation ID. A retry count without a delay can hammer the same failed dependency.

Tabletop exercise before project decisions

In weekly-logs/week-10.md, predict the outcome at three crash points: before the write, after the write but before status update, and after status update but before acknowledgment. Check which side effect may repeat at each point. Then fill the queue section of projects/architecture-review/reliability.md. No queue is started by this exercise.

Hint 1

Ask what durable evidence survives the worker. Memory and logs alone should not decide whether to repeat a business side effect.

Hint 2 and explained result

Before the write, retry can perform the work. After the write, stable-key upsert should converge; an unkeyed insert may duplicate. After recorded success, duplicate delivery should discover the stored result. Separate writes across systems still have partial-failure windows; name them and define reconciliation.

Design a replay that a tired operator can follow

A dead-job replay needs an owner, a diagnosis, a corrected cause, and a check that the operation remains valid. Preserve original ID and attempt history; decide whether replay uses the same idempotency key. Never “fix” a poison document by unlimited retries. Stop replay if error rate rises or tenant/version validation fails.

Begin the dead-job section of runbook.md: trigger, read-only inspection, safe mitigation, verification, rollback and escalation owner. Use role names without contacting anyone. Evidence is your crash-point table and a dry run against a fictional job, not a live Redis assertion.

Variation: two workers claim the same job concurrently. Explain why “check then insert” can race and why a unique constraint/atomic claim matters. If transactions are a gap, use only the relevant part of the earlier transaction lesson. Continue to Lesson 7.
