Week 10 / Lesson 05

Bound a remote failure

Choose retries using deadlines, repeatability and observable failure.

A timeout limits how long a caller waits. It does not prove the remote operation never happened. A retry is another attempt; it may recover a temporary failure, amplify an overload, or duplicate a side effect. First decide whether repeating the operation is safe.

Trace a bounded read

Read retry_read in the completed example. The supplied read fails once with TimeoutError, succeeds next, and returns “available.” range(3) gives attempt numbers 0, 1, 2. The except handles only that exception; raise with no argument preserves it on the last attempt. A ValueError escapes immediately. This small example deliberately omits real waiting and jitter; do not paste it around production calls.

A real policy needs a total deadline, bounded attempts and delay between attempts. Suppose your design grants retrieval 500 ms of a 2 s request budget: two 150 ms attempts with at most 100 ms backoff leave roughly 100 ms margin for overhead. These are exercise numbers, not a recommendation for your real service. Use remaining budget, include connection and response time, honor provider retry guidance, and avoid retrying at every layer. Three outer attempts each triggering three inner attempts can create nine downstream calls.

Exponential backoff increases delays between repeated failures; jitter randomizes them so clients do not all retry together. Retry a transient timeout/503 only if the operation is repeatable. Treat 429 as a quota signal, respect any retry delay and your deadline. Invalid input, failed authorization and an unrecognized tool should be corrected or rejected, not retried.

Guided exercise

From the weekly root, edit retryable in practice/exercises.py. Return a Boolean for the contract's small policy, then run:

python -m unittest discover -s practice -p test_exercises.py -k retry -v
Hint 1

Two independent conditions must hold: transient status and permission to repeat. Join them with and.

Hint 2 and explained answer
return status in (429, 503) and safe_to_repeat

The tuple is just a list of allowed statuses. An unsafe operation is rejected even when its status is transient. This exercise classifies; it does not schedule retries.

Timeouts and circuit breakers

The async example uses wait_for to cancel a cooperative task when a short deadline expires. A blocking function can prevent timely cancellation; SDK connection/read timeouts still matter. A circuit breaker remembers recent failure: closed permits traffic; open fails quickly; half-open permits limited probes. It can reduce repeated pressure on a sick dependency but needs recovery rules and shared-state reasoning. Awareness is enough this week; adding one without evidence creates another failure mode.

Write a retrieval timeout policy in reliability.md: user-visible result, deadline, maximum attempts, safe errors, logging, recovery. Avoid returning “no matches” for unavailable retrieval; it misdiagnoses infrastructure as content absence. Evidence: passing exercise plus a narrated failure trace in the weekly log. Variation: the provider times out after accepting a billable generation request. Explain why a second call may cost money even if no answer was received. Continue to Lesson 6.
